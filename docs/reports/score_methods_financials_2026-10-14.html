Financials scored within their industry, beside the ex-Financials control

The strict sector-neutral sort of docs/reports/score_methods_sector_neutral_2026-10-14.html run three ways: scoring within the sector as shipped; scoring a Financials member within its industry group (banks, insurance, REITs, financial services, by SIC code) with the sort still within the sector; and the academic control that drops Financials and Real Estate before scoring. Generated 2026-10-14 from the database by tools/score_methods.py --variants. Nothing here was written to the database.

Where Financials members land. In the S&P 1500, 26% of bank company-months sat in the sector's bottom fifth and 2% in its top under sector-relative scoring; industry-relative, 19% and 19%. Section 1 has every group and universe.

The S&P 1500 composite, monthly, AQR scoring: sector-relative -1.2% (alpha t -0.63) equal-weighted, -1.7% (t -0.37) cap-weighted; industry-relative -1.5% (alpha t -0.70) equal-weighted, -2.2% (t -0.52) cap-weighted; ex-Financials and Real Estate -1.7% (alpha t -0.63) equal-weighted, -1.7% (t -0.37) cap-weighted.

What the three variants are, exactly

  • Sector-relative: every measure standardised within its (date, GICS sector) group, the composite the mean of a member's available scores re-standardised within the sector, quintiles within the sector. The shipped construction.
  • Industry-relative: for members of Financials, every measure standardised within the (date, industry group) instead, the group by the filer's SIC code (sec_reference.company.sic_latest: banks 6000–6099, insurance 6300–6499, REITs 6798, financial services otherwise); the composite, the mean of those group-relative scores, re-standardised within the sector as before, which leaves the order within the sector unchanged; the sort unchanged, quintiles within the sector. A group with fewer than 10 members on a date, or a member without a SIC, is scored within the sector as before. REITs are a group only while the dated panel files them under Financials, before the 2016-09 GICS change made Real Estate a sector. Every other sector is scored as in the sector-relative variant.
  • ex-Financials and Real Estate: both sectors dropped before scoring, the GICS form of the academic rule that excludes SIC 6000–6999 (Fama and French 1992, Novy-Marx 2013). Everything else unchanged.

The SIC code is the one on the company's latest filing; a bank is a bank throughout, so for this purpose it is stable. Scoring groups by industry instead of sector is what systematic managers do when a sector mixes balance-sheet businesses with asset-light ones; the industry-specific descriptors they add (return on tangible equity, efficiency ratio, combined ratio, funds from operations) are not built here, so a bank is still scored on the measures the panel has, three of which divide by assets.

6. What this does and does not settle

  • It separates the groups; it does not change what a bank is scored on. A bank still has no gross profit and no operating margin in the panel, and three of its seven measures divide by assets. The next two steps, not built here: score banks and insurers only on the measures that mean the same thing across financials (return on equity, its quarterly form, revenue growth, earnings surprise), an allowlist rule by SIC prefix; then the descriptors their own analysts use (return on tangible equity, pre-provision profit to assets, efficiency ratio, provisions to loans, the combined ratio, funds from operations for REITs), each a formula variant keyed by SIC prefix in the concept layer.
  • Survivors only. Unchanged from the sector-neutral page: no delisted company has a price series, so every short leg here is flattered.
  • Real Estate has the same shape. Sorted across the whole universe, Real Estate members land in the bottom fifth far more often than the top; sector neutrality handles that, and inside the sector nearly every member is a REIT, so an industry split does nothing there. Funds from operations is the fix.
  • Cap weighting rests on guarded share counts. 4 company-dates held more than 15% of the panel's cap on their date, a share count the ladder's guards could not refuse or a split between the price's month end and the cover page, and are left out of the cap-weighted legs: 1067983 2012-05-31 (92%), 1067983 2012-06-29 (92%), 1067983 2012-07-31 (92%), GOOG 2022-07-29 (44%).
  • Dropping the sectors is a control, not a construction. The ex-Financials variant says how much of the sector-neutral result the two sectors carried; it is not a recommendation to run without them.
