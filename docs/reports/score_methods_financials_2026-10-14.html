Financials: industry-relative scoring, comparable measures, and the ex-Financials control

The strict sector-neutral sort of docs/reports/score_methods_sector_neutral_2026-10-14.html run 5 ways: scoring within the sector as shipped; scoring a Financials member within its industry group (banks, insurance, REITs, financial services, by SIC code) with the sort still within the sector; scoring banks, brokers and insurers only on the measures that mean the same thing across financials; both together; and the academic control that drops Financials and Real Estate before scoring. Each is a switch of tools/score_methods.py, applied at run time to the stored panel. Generated 2026-10-14 from the database by tools/score_methods.py --variants. Nothing here was written to the database.

What the variants are, exactly

  • Sector-relative: every measure standardised within its (date, GICS sector) group, the composite the mean of a member's available scores re-standardised within the sector, quintiles within the sector. The shipped construction.
  • Industry-relative: for members of Financials, every measure standardised within the (date, industry group) instead, the group by the filer's SIC code (sec_reference.company.sic_latest: banks 6000–6099, insurance 6300–6499, REITs 6798, financial services otherwise); the composite, the mean of those group-relative scores, re-standardised within the sector as before, which leaves the order within the sector unchanged; the sort unchanged, quintiles within the sector. A group with fewer than 10 members on a date, or a member without a SIC, is scored within the sector as before. REITs are a group only while the dated panel files them under Financials, before the 2016-09 GICS change made Real Estate a sector. Every other sector is scored as in the sector-relative variant.
  • Comparable measures: a member with SIC 6000–6499 (depository and non-depository credit, brokers and exchanges, insurance carriers and agents) is scored only on return on equity, quarterly return on equity, revenue growth and earnings surprise; gross profit to assets, cash flow to assets, both returns on assets and the operating margin are left out for it before scoring, and its composite needs 3 of those four where every other member needs 4 of nine. It therefore does not appear in the five single-factor sorts it has no value for, and a sector left with fewer than 10 scored members on a date drops out of that sort. Scoring stays within the sector.
  • Industry-relative, comparable measures: both of the above.
  • ex-Financials and Real Estate: both sectors dropped before scoring, the GICS form of the academic rule that excludes SIC 6000–6999 (Fama and French 1992, Novy-Marx 2013). Everything else unchanged.

The SIC code is the one on the company's latest filing; a bank is a bank throughout, so for this purpose it is stable. Scoring groups by industry instead of sector, and scoring a bank only on what a bank's accounts can say, are what systematic managers do when a sector mixes balance-sheet businesses with asset-light ones; the industry-specific descriptors they add (return on tangible equity, efficiency ratio, combined ratio, funds from operations) are not built here yet.

6. What this does and does not settle

  • Separating the groups and restricting the measures are the first two steps; the third is not built. A bank scored on comparable measures is still scored on what the panel has. The descriptors its own analysts use (return on tangible equity, pre-provision profit to assets, efficiency ratio, provisions to loans, the combined ratio, funds from operations for REITs) are each a formula variant keyed by SIC prefix in the concept layer, and that is the next step.
  • Survivors only. Unchanged from the sector-neutral page: no delisted company has a price series, so every short leg here is flattered.
  • Real Estate has the same shape. Sorted across the whole universe, Real Estate members land in the bottom fifth far more often than the top; sector neutrality handles that, and inside the sector nearly every member is a REIT, so an industry split does nothing there. Funds from operations is the fix.
  • Cap weighting rests on guarded share counts. 4 company-dates held more than 15% of the panel's cap on their date, a share count the ladder's guards could not refuse or a split between the price's month end and the cover page, and are left out of the cap-weighted legs: 1067983 2012-05-31 (92%), 1067983 2012-06-29 (92%), 1067983 2012-07-31 (92%), GOOG 2022-07-29 (44%).
  • Dropping the sectors is a control, not a construction. The ex-Financials variant says how much of the sector-neutral result the two sectors carried; it is not a recommendation to run without them.
