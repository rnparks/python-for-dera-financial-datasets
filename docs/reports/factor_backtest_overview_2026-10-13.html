4. What the S&P 1500 panel is

Not a fourth index history: on each month end that all three component panels hold, every S&P 500, 400 and 600 member is taken with the factor values already stored for it, one row per company, and the index percentiles and the composite are recomputed over all of them by the sheet's rule (sec_market.build_factor_panel_union). A date any component lacks is refused, so the union begins where the S&P 600 history does, 2018-08. Because the percentiles are over the whole 1,500, a sort now spans sizes: the composite's annual sort loads -0.42 on SMB, so without a size control part of any spread here is a bet on small versus large. A size-neutral version (sort within size terciles, or value-weight the buckets) is the next step for this universe, not more factors.

5. Why the spreads are biased down, not up

  • Survivors only. Yahoo returns nothing for a delisted ticker, so 718 of the 2,417 companies ever in the S&P 1500 have no prices. Failures and takeovers leave an index from the bottom quintile more than the top; their missing returns flatter Q1 and shrink the measured spread. The share of members with a return the following month in the first panel year: S&P 500 66% (2010), S&P 400 55% (2011), S&P 600 65% (2018), S&P 1500 74% (2018). The small caps are the most flattered.
  • Sixteen months of frozen small-cap membership. The S&P 600 page is unusable from 2019-10 to 2021-02, so the 600 and the union carry the 444 members seen on both sides of the gap rather than 600 (596 just before, 599 just after): fewer, more persistent companies in 2020.
  • Equal weight, no size control. Every bucket is equal-weighted, which overweights the smallest members of each universe, and the union sorts across sizes.
  • 120 tests. At the 5% level, 6 |t| above 2 are expected from noise alone.

6. The database: fixed for good, and open

Fixed structurally (2026-10-13)

Suspect facts. 5,546 facts dated after their own filing (1,047 filers; Catalent's nine-month income dated 2024-03-31 in a February filing) and 87 balance-sheet lines mis-scaled by a thousand or a million (Hill-Rom's FY2021 total assets of 4,999.1 dollars) are refused by every gold table through sec_gold.suspect_fact, decided from the filing alone so a flag never changes later. The quarterly fold refreshes the list before the fact tables. Check 70.

The one-transaction build. Every matview analyses itself as it is built and after every refresh; the peer-statistics predicate is a join. Eleven hours became 48 minutes.

One row per company. The factor sheet cannot count a company twice through a ticker handoff (IR/TT 2020); check 71 pins the three index-months.

The sector every page left blank. Filled by rule as a label, with the proof that every membership interval kept its key (6,342), that the members and tickers on every month end since 2008 are identical (239,662 member-months), and that no company's own peer values moved (55,496 sector rows added, 0 same-key values changed). The S&P 400 panel carries the composite for 377 members a date in 2017 and the S&P 600 for 509 in 2018, from none. Check 73: 0 foreign labels, 138 unclassified intervals.

The backtest at 1,500 names. Thirteen minutes a call became seconds, the S&P 500 numbers unchanged to the digit.

Banks judged by the filing's SIC (check 63), so a registrant that changes industry later cannot fail a historical test.

Open, with its evidence

Survivorship. 718 delisted members have no prices (1,864 of 1,868 listed symbols answered on 2026-10-12; CUK, HCIIP, SATS and TDDWW did not). Polygon's flat files or CRSP close it.

Seats keyed by registrant. A registrant that absorbed another member and kept the seat collapses both into one: BancorpSouth is absent from the S&P 400 between 2018-10-19 and 2021-11-02; the recycled ticker BKR gives Michael Baker the S&P 500 seat from 2017-07-07 to 2019-12; Ashland LLC reappears for 2018-10-19 to 2019-10-02. The fix is a seat keyed by ticker before the CIK-succession cut.

The S&P 600 captures of 2019-10 to 2021-02 carry an extra table and are unusable: membership through the gap is frozen at 444.

Ticker handoffs are dated at the crosswalk's next capture, not from the filing that announces them.

Value weights and a size control are not built; the backtest is equal-weight only.

7. Recommendation

  1. Close survivorship first. Load delisted members' prices from Polygon's flat files or CRSP. It is the one change that can move the conclusion: the bottom buckets are missing exactly the companies that failed, and the 600 is missing a third of its 2018 members' returns.
  2. Then a size control and value weights in sec_market.backtest, before reading anything into the 1,500: sort within size terciles and value-weight the buckets, so a spread is not a bet on small versus large.
  3. Then the reference layer: seats keyed by ticker before the succession cut, and the 2019 to 2021 S&P 600 captures parsed for what they are. Both have clear proofs (membership counts by date, the named companies above).
  4. Not more factors. Nine accounting factors and a composite found nothing in four universes; a tenth will not. What would be informative is the same sorts with delisting returns in, and value-weighted.
