Do accounting factors pay in the S&P 500?

Point-in-time fundamentals from SEC DERA, month-end prices, equal-weight quintile sorts rebalanced monthly, quarterly and annually, measured raw and against the Fama-French five factors and momentum. Generated 2026-10-13 from the database by tools/factor_report.py.

Verdict: nothing pays beyond chance. 1 of 30 sorts clear t 2 after the five factors and momentum, which is what 30 tests produce from noise. Across the composite and nine factors at three rebalancing frequencies, no top-minus-bottom spread has a raw t-statistic above 1.6. After the five-factor model the profitability sorts are largely their factor loadings: 0.30 to 0.56 on profitability (RMW), -0.68 to -0.21 on value (HML), and 0.07 to 0.30 on momentum for the quarterly signals. 1 of 30 tests clear t 2 on FF5 (Revenue growth (low is good) at quarterly rebalancing, 4.3% a year, t 2.50) and 1 on FF5 plus momentum (Revenue growth (low is good) at quarterly rebalancing, 4.2% a year, t 2.37). With 30 tests, one or two |t| above 2 are what chance delivers at the 5% level.

The machinery checks out: the equal-weight benchmark of all members earns 13.8% a year with an FF5 alpha of -0.3%, market beta 0.98 and R² 0.96. The Yahoo returns and the French series line up.

4. Why the spreads are biased down, not up

  • Survivors only. Yahoo returns nothing for a delisted ticker, so 718 of the 2,417 companies ever in the S&P 1500 have no prices. Failures and takeovers leave the index from the bottom quintile more than the top; their missing returns flatter Q1 and shrink the measured spread. The share of panel members with a price the following month rises from 66.1% in 2010 to 97.8% by 2025.
  • Large caps, equal-weighted. The published profitability premium is smallest among the largest stocks; an equal-weight sort within the S&P 500 is one of the harder tests to pass.
  • The first year is thinner. The composite covers 290 of 498 members in 2010 (the surprise factor needs eight quarters of history and DERA begins in 2009) and 477 or more a date from 2013.
  • 30 tests. Ten sorts on three schedules; at the 5% level one or two |t| > 2 are expected from noise alone.

6. Method, exactly

  • Universe. sec_reference.index_members('SP500', date): the constituents on each month end from the replayed index history, one row per company (a seat whose ticker the crosswalk still assigns to its previous holder cannot count a company twice; check 71).
  • Factors. sec_gold.factor_sheet_asof, stored per month end in sec_market.factor_panel. Annual factors come from the newest fiscal year filed by the date (not older than 550 days), quarterly ones from the newest quarter filed by the date (not older than 200 days). Every input is keyed on tradable_from, the first session a filing could be traded on, never the filing date; 57% of filings are accepted after the close. The panel's CHECK constraints refuse any row whose period end or availability is after its own knowledge date.
  • Suspect facts. 5,546 facts whose period ends after their own filing date and 87 balance-sheet lines printed in thousands or millions without saying so are refused by every gold table (sec_gold.suspect_fact), decided from the filing and earlier filings only.
  • Prices. Yahoo Finance month-end closes for the primary ticker of each surviving member (sec_market.monthly_price; 1,864 of 1,868 symbols answered on 2026-10-12). Returns use the dividend-and-split-adjusted close; a month is skipped across a gap of more than 35 days, when either month did not trade, or when the prior as-traded close was under a dollar.
  • Sort and hold. sec_market.backtest: on each rebalance date, NTILE(5) over the signed percentile among members that have the factor; equal weight within bucket; held through every month end up to and including the next rebalance; a member without a price that month drops out of its bucket for that month.
  • Statistics. sec_market.backtest_summary for means, t, Sharpe and hit rates; tools/ff_baseline.py for the regressions (Newey-West, six lags) on Ken French's monthly five factors and momentum, percent returns read as decimals.

7. What the build caught along the way

  • The sector every page left blank. The S&P 400 page from 2013-10 to 2019-08 and the S&P 600 page before 2021-03 gave each member a sub-industry and no sector, and the sector is the level the cross-sections rank in, so the S&P 400 had no annual factor at all in 2017 and the S&P 600 none before 2021. The spine now fills the sector by rule (the sub-industry's sector, else the sector the company carries under another label), as a label only: every membership interval kept its key, the members and tickers on every month end since 2008 are identical, and no company's own values moved. The S&P 500 page always carried its sector, so this panel was unaffected; three of its dates were rebuilt after the fill and came out byte-identical.
  • A company counted twice. The panel's primary key refused 2020-03-31: the S&P 500's IR and TT seats both resolved to Trane Technologies, because the ticker IR passed to Ingersoll Rand Inc on 2020-03-03 and the crosswalk moves a ticker at its next capture (2020-06-28). Three month ends, one company, the only such case since 2010; the sheet is now one row per company and check 71 pins the count.
  • Thirteen minutes a call. The backtest resolved the as-of ticker inside the price join, once per member-month; on the 1,500-name panel a call ran past thirteen minutes. It now resolves each member's ticker once per rebalance date, the S&P 500 numbers are unchanged to the digit, and a call takes seconds.
  • A planner pathology. The gold layer is rebuilt in one transaction, so a freshly built matview has no statistics until commit. The peer-statistics build costed a correlated EXISTS as free and ran it as a per-row index scan for eleven hours before it was cancelled. The predicate is now a join, every matview is analysed as soon as it is indexed, and the rebuild takes 48 minutes.
  • Look-ahead by mis-dating. Catalent's 10-Q of 2024-02-14 dates a nine-month net income to 2024-03-31; the panel's CHECK caught it, and such facts are now refused at the source (5,546 facts of 1,047 filers). The quarterly fold refreshes the list before the fact tables, so a new quarter's mis-dated facts are caught the same way.
  • A scale error at the top of every sort. Hill-Rom's FY2021 10-K carries total assets of 4,999.1 dollars against 4,671.1 million for FY2020 in the same filing; it had produced a return on assets of 49,709. Refused with 87 such lines from 8 filers.

8. Open

  • Survivorship. Delisted members have no prices: 718 of the 2,417 companies ever in the S&P 1500, and in this universe 66.1% of 2010 members have a return the following month. Polygon's flat files or CRSP close the gap; nothing else changes the conclusion as much. Four open symbols Yahoo lacked: CUK, HCIIP, SATS, TDDWW.
  • Seats keyed by registrant, not by ticker. A registrant that absorbed another index member and kept the seat collapses both into one from the capture both appear under: BancorpSouth (1299939, which took Cadence and its ticker in 2021-11) is absent from the S&P 400 between 2018-10-19 and 2021-11-02; SEC's succession row for BKR is a recycled ticker, not a succession, and gives Michael Baker the S&P 500 seat from 2017-07-07 to 2019-12; Ashland LLC reappears for 2018-10-19 to 2019-10-02 between two Ashland Inc intervals. The fix is a seat keyed by ticker before the cut.
  • The S&P 600 captures of 2019-10 to 2021-02 carry an extra table and are unusable, so membership through the gap is the 444 members seen on both sides (596 before, 599 after). Parsing those pages properly restores the leavers and joiners of sixteen months.
  • Ticker handoffs are dated at the crosswalk's next capture rather than from the filing that announces them (the IR/TT case above).
  • Value-weighted buckets and a size control. The backtest is equal-weight only; across the 1,500 a sort without a size control is partly a bet on small versus large.
