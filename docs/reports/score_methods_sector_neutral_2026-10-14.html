Three ways to score, one sector-neutral sort

The same nine point-in-time accounting factors and composite, scored the AQR way, the MSCI way and the Barra way within each sector, sorted long the top fifth and short the bottom fifth of every sector, measured raw and against Fama-French five factors plus momentum. Generated 2026-10-14 from the database by tools/score_methods.py. Nothing here was written to the database.

The choice of scoring method does not change the answer. For a single factor the three scores order a sector's members identically, so the nine single-factor sorts are the same under all three by construction; only the composite, where the scale of each score decides how the nine combine, can differ, and it differs little. 1. The three methods, exactly

  • AQR (Asness, Frazzini and Pedersen 2019, Quality Minus Junk): within each (date, sector) group each factor is converted to ranks and the ranks are standardised to a z-score; the composite is the mean of a member's available scores (four or more), standardised again within the group. Ranks make the score immune to outliers, which is the paper's stated reason.
  • MSCI (MSCI Quality Indexes Methodology, May 2022, as in its sector-neutral variant): within each group the raw value is winsorised at the 5th and 95th percentile ranks, then z-scored with the group's mean and standard deviation; the composite is the simple average of the z-scores. Magnitude survives: a company far ahead on a ratio scores further ahead.
  • Barra (MSCI FaCS / Barra fundamental factor models): within each group the raw value is winsorised at the mean ± 3 standard deviations, then standardised; descriptors combined linearly and the combination re-standardised. Two departures here, because the panel carries no market capitalisation: the mean is equal-weighted where Barra uses a cap-weighted mean, and the winsorisation is a single pass where Barra iterates a robust mean.

Why the single-factor results coincide: within a (date, sector) group, the rank, the 5/95-winsorised value and the 3 sd-winsorised value are all non-decreasing functions of the raw value, and the clipping only ties members already inside the top or bottom fifth, so each method puts the same members in each quintile. The methods differ in the composite because a z-score of ranks is bounded and evenly spaced while a z-score of winsorised values keeps the distance between members, so a company far ahead on one factor pulls further ahead on the average.

Everything else is held fixed across the three: the signed factor values from sec_market.factor_panel (revenue growth negated, so a higher score always means the higher expected return in the literature); sectors with fewer than 10 scored members on a date left out; within each sector five equal buckets by score; equal weight within a bucket; the sector's spread its top fifth minus its bottom fifth; the universe spread the sector spreads weighted by member count, so the long and short sides carry identical sector counts; holding to the next rebalance date with the ticker the spine assigns on the rebalance date, exactly as sec_market.backtest does; alphas from a regression of the monthly spread on the French factors with Newey-West (six lags) standard errors; French vintage 202608.

5. What to read into this, and what not to

  • Survivors only. Prices exist only for tickers still listed, so the bottom quintile is missing the names that failed or were bought, and their absent returns flatter the short leg. This biases every spread here downward and is the agreed next data step; no scoring method can repair it.
  • One regime. The profitability sorts were flat to slightly positive through 2024 and lost heavily from 2025, when the Fama-French profitability factor itself reversed. The three methods inherit that equally.
  • Ranks and winsorised values order companies almost identically. The agreement correlations above are the measurement; the methods differ in whether magnitude beyond order carries information, and in these universes it carries none that survives the factor models.
  • Equal weights, no size control. Within a bucket every company counts the same; the long-short carries a negative market beta and a small-cap tilt that the factor models absorb and the raw spreads do not.
