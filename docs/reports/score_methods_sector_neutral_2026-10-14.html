The choice of scoring method does not change the answer. For a single factor the three scores order a sector's members identically, so the nine single-factor sorts are the same under all three by construction; only the composite, where the scale of each score decides how the nine combine, can differ, and it differs little. 1. The three methods and the three weightings, exactly

  • AQR (Asness, Frazzini and Pedersen 2019, Quality Minus Junk): within each (date, sector) group each factor is converted to ranks and the ranks are standardised to a z-score; the composite is the mean of a member's available scores (four or more), standardised again within the group. Ranks make the score immune to outliers, which is the paper's stated reason.
  • MSCI (MSCI Quality Indexes Methodology, May 2022, as in its sector-neutral variant): within each group the raw value is winsorised at the 5th and 95th percentile ranks, then z-scored with the group's mean and standard deviation; the composite is the simple average of the z-scores. Magnitude survives: a company far ahead on a ratio scores further ahead.
  • Barra (MSCI FaCS / Barra fundamental factor models): within each group the raw value is winsorised at the mean ± 3 standard deviations, then standardised; descriptors combined linearly and the combination re-standardised. Two departures here: the mean is equal-weighted where Barra uses a cap-weighted mean, and the winsorisation is a single pass where Barra iterates a robust mean.

Why the single-factor results coincide: within a (date, sector) group, the rank, the 5/95-winsorised value and the 3 sd-winsorised value are all non-decreasing functions of the raw value, and the clipping only ties members already inside the top or bottom fifth, so each method puts the same members in each quintile. The methods differ in the composite because a z-score of ranks is bounded and evenly spaced while a z-score of winsorised values keeps the distance between members, so a company far ahead on one factor pulls further ahead on the average.

  • Equal-weighted (the headline): every stock in a sector's top fifth has the same weight, so does every stock in its bottom fifth; the sector spreads are averaged with member-count weights. That is an equal-weighted long book against an equal-weighted short book with the sector mix matched on both sides.
  • Cap-weighted: within each sector's top fifth and bottom fifth stocks are weighted by market cap on the rebalance date (sec_market.market_cap_at: the month-end close before the date times the cover-page shares knowable on it); the sector spreads are averaged with each sector's share of the universe's market cap that date, the same weights on both sides. A member without a market cap, 21.0% of company-dates, mostly early years before a cover-page share count is knowable, drops out of the cap-weighted legs only.
  • Equal sectors: as the headline but every sector's spread counts the same, utilities as much as industrials; shown for the composite only, since small sectors make it noisy.

Everything else is held fixed: the signed factor values from sec_market.factor_panel (revenue growth negated, so a higher score always means the higher expected return in the literature); sectors with fewer than 10 scored members on a date left out; within each sector five equal buckets by score; holding to the next rebalance date with the ticker the spine assigns on the rebalance date, exactly as sec_market.backtest does; alphas from a regression of the monthly spread on the French factors with Newey-West (six lags) standard errors; French vintage 202608.

6. What to read into this, and what not to

  • Survivors only. Prices exist only for tickers still listed, so the bottom quintile is missing the names that failed or were bought, and their absent returns flatter the short leg. This biases every spread here downward and is the agreed next data step; no scoring method or weighting repairs it.
  • One regime. The profitability sorts were flat to slightly positive through 2024 and lost from 2025-01, when the Fama-French profitability factor itself reversed. The three methods inherit that equally; the two weightings inherit it in different sizes, which section 3 measures.
  • Ranks and winsorised values order companies almost identically. The agreement correlations are the measurement; the methods differ in whether magnitude beyond order carries information, and in these universes it carries none that survives the factor models.
  • Cap weighting is point-in-time but approximate. One price for every class, the primary ticker's, so a dual-class issuer with a differently priced second class is approximate; and a member with no knowable share count on the date is absent from the cap-weighted legs.
